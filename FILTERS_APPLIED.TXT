FILTERS APPLIED ACROSS THE NOTEBOOKS — WHAT, WHERE, WHY
=========================================================

Three data-generation problems were found in DataCoSupplyChainDataset.csv while
building this archive. This file records which notebooks needed a fix because
of which problem, what the fix was, and what it changed. Full technical
detail for each problem lives in the notebook named next to it below.

THE THREE PROBLEMS
-------------------

1. ORDER-COMPOSITION ARTIFACT (Oct 2017 onward)
   Found in: 0.1_company_growth.ipynb §2
   From Nov 2017 on, every order collapses to exactly 1 line item (vs ~3.0
   before) and per-line Sales/profit shift to a different, higher average —
   a different data-generation regime, not a change in the business. Order
   counts, items-per-order, and revenue-per-order stop being comparable
   across this boundary. Oct 2017 is used as the cutoff everywhere (not Nov)
   to match the convention 0.1 and 0.6 already established.

2. FAKE CUSTOMER IDs (Oct 2017 onward)
   Found in: 0.8_consumer_base_analysis.ipynb §2
   8,322 of 20,652 Order Customer Ids (40%) appear ONLY from Oct 2017 on, and
   100% of those have exactly one lifetime order — a byproduct of problem #1
   (every split single-item order looks like a new one-time customer). This
   alone inflates the naive one-time-customer rate to 43% vs. the true 4.6%.

3. REGION-ROTATION ARTIFACT (dataset-wide, not just Oct 2017+)
   Found in: 0.5_by_region_analysis.ipynb §2, 0.7_region_activity_gaps.ipynb
   19 of the 23 `Order Region`s go permanently dark for months at a time —
   most only have 5-6 consecutive active months out of the ~36-month
   dataset, then never appear again. No region has continuous coverage.
   This is NOT fixable with a date cutoff (it isn't confined to one window),
   so it's handled differently from #1 and #2 below.


NOTEBOOK-BY-NOTEBOOK
---------------------

0_data_overview.ipynb
  Filter applied: NONE (by design)
  Why: this notebook's whole purpose is to show the raw data as-is, artifacts
  included — it's the source that later notebooks cite. Filtering it would
  hide the very things it exists to surface.

0.1_company_growth.ipynb
  Filter applied: date cutoff (Oct 2017+) for growth/trend figures — already
  in place, this is the notebook that discovered problem #1.
  Why: full-year and quarterly growth numbers looked like a business
  collapse in 2017 Q4 / 2018 Q1 until traced to the artifact.
  No changes made this pass.

0.2_sales_corr.ipynb
  Filter applied:
    (a) date cutoff (Oct 2017+, problem #1) before the region / discount /
        category correlation sections (§2-§4). The page-views-vs-purchases
        section (§1) is deliberately EXEMPT — it's scoped to the clickstream
        log's own Sep 2017-Jan 2018 window (the only period that file
        covers), and compares line-item-level counts, which aren't distorted
        by problem #1 the way order-level counts are.
    (b) added a caveat note (problem #3, region-rotation) next to the
        region-ranked margin table, since region totals still reflect very
        unequal active windows even after (a).
  Why: §2-§4 aggregate over the full order history; without the cutoff a
  region/category's ranking could shift just because more of its
  differently-shaped tail-end orders survived in the raw file.
  Numeric impact: none of the Takeaways cite specific figures, so no prose
  needed correcting.

0.3_prod_category_analyzis.ipynb
  Filter applied: date cutoff (Oct 2017+, problem #1), applied right after
  load, before all 50-category rollups.
  Why: category totals shouldn't be higher or lower just because a category
  happened to have more of its tail-end (differently-shaped) orders survive
  in the raw file.
  Numeric impact: discount-rate spread shifted from 9.3%-10.9% to 9.34%-
  10.94% (unchanged, wasn't worth rewriting). Corrected a stale sentence in
  the intro that had claimed category sums were "unaffected" by the artifact
  — they are computed after exclusion now, consistent with the rest of the
  notebook.

0.4_prod_region_cross.ipynb
  Filter applied:
    (a) date cutoff (Oct 2017+, problem #1).
    (b) added a `months_active` column to the region leaderboard (problem
        #3, region-rotation) — can't fix unequal exposure with a row filter,
        so the column makes it visible instead.
  Why + impact: this notebook cross-tabs region x category, so both
  problems hit it directly. The date cutoff had a much bigger knock-on
  effect here than anywhere else: 18 categories (including Computers,
  Cameras, Crafts) dropped below the "10 order lines in at least one region"
  threshold entirely and disappeared from the naive-vs-weighted-margin
  analysis (32 categories qualify now, not 50). This rewrote two of the
  notebook's headline examples:
    - "Computers in Oceania" (a lone outlier cell) no longer exists in the
      data shown — replaced with "Golf Shoes in West Asia" (-33.9% margin
      against a column that's otherwise +9% to +27%).
    - Basketball used to be the naive-average illusion (rank 9 -> 47 once
      weighted). It's now dead last on BOTH naive and weighted rank (32 of
      32) — it's genuinely negative-margin, not a coverage artifact. Lacrosse
      took over as the naive-average illusion example instead (rank 5 -> 18).
    - Cardio Equipment's finding survives (still sold in all 23 regions,
      still drops sharply once weighted) but the exact numbers moved: rank
      9 -> 26 (was 19 -> 37), a 17-rank drop (was 18).

0.5_by_region_analysis.ipynb
  Filter applied: date cutoff (Oct 2017+, problem #1) for the profit/revenue/
  margin ranking (§1) and every `pct_of_company_profit` figure. Deliberately
  NOT applied to the activity-window table in §2 (`active_start`,
  `active_end`, `days_active`, `months_with_orders`) — that section's whole
  point is measuring true region activity (problem #3), so it uses the full
  unfiltered data on purpose. The notebook now carries both `df` (filtered)
  and `df_full` (raw) for this reason.
  Why + impact: this is the notebook that selects the TOP3/BOT3 regions
  feeding every 0.5.x deep dive, so getting it right mattered most. The
  bottom 3 (Central Asia, Canada, Southern Africa) were unaffected — their
  data ends before Jan 2017, well before the cutoff. The top 3 SET didn't
  change, but the ORDER did: Central America overtakes Western Europe for
  #1 by profit $ ($616K vs $551K) once Western Europe's Oct-Nov 2017 tail is
  excluded. Company total profit used as the denominator for every
  `pct_of_company_profit` figure dropped from $3.97M to $3.69M (excluded
  rows were disproportionately profitable per-line, so this isn't just
  proportional to the 4.7% of rows removed). Rewrote the ranking table intro,
  the "$1.58M / 39.8%" and "$67.8K / 1.7%" combined-group figures (now
  $1.50M / 40.8% and $67.8K / 1.8%), and the notebook table linking to
  0.5.1-0.5.8 to reflect the new #1/#2 order.

0.5.1_deepdive_western_europe.ipynb

0.6_review_by_quarter.ipynb
  Filter applied: NONE this pass — already handled differently and
  correctly. Uses a "flag, don't drop" approach: every quarter (including
  Q4 2017+) stays in the levels view, tagged `artifact_affected`, because
  0.6's own finding is that profit $ and margin % (unlike revenue-per-order)
  aren't meaningfully distorted by problem #1 — so dropping those quarters
  would throw away real, valid figures. This is a deliberately different
  convention from 0.2-0.5.x's "exclude" approach: 0.6 is a complete-quarters
  levels report where every quarter needs to appear; 0.2-0.5.x produce
  rankings/comparisons used to decide which regions or categories get
  attention, where blending in a known-different regime is the bigger risk.

0.7_region_activity_gaps.ipynb
  Filter applied: NONE (by design) — this notebook exists specifically to
  investigate problem #3 (region-rotation) using the full, unfiltered
  timeline; filtering it would remove the evidence it's built to show.

0.8_consumer_base_analysis.ipynb / 0.8.1_deepdive_top15pct.ipynb
  Filter applied: date cutoff (Sep 2017, problem #2) — already in place,
  these are the notebooks that discovered the fake-customer-ID artifact.
  No changes made this pass.


0.9_exposure_adjusted_region_growth.ipynb
  Filter applied: date cutoff (Oct 2017+, problem #1), same convention as
  0.2-0.5.x. Also introduces its own "noise-floor" filter (a region's active
  block excluded from block-to-block comparison if it averages <100 orders/
  month) to catch a further wrinkle problem #3 produces: the 3 Europe
  regions and Eastern Asia don't go fully to zero between their real active
  blocks, they drop to a 1%-of-normal trickle (27-32 orders/month for
  Europe, 15/month for Eastern Asia) that lands exactly inside the 9-region
  Aug2016-Jan2017 cohort's window -- read as cross-region leakage, not a
  second real season, and excluded from the growth comparison on that basis.
  Why + impact: this notebook asks a new question (growth within a region's
  own active window, not just its level) that only 8 of 23 regions can
  answer at all after the Oct-2017 cutoff (15 have exactly one clean window
  and no second one to compare it to -- Oceania and Southeast Asia's only
  second burst falls entirely inside the excluded Nov2017-Jan2018 window).
  Of those 8, one more (Eastern Asia) drops out on the noise-floor filter,
  leaving 7 regions with a genuine two-window comparison. Headline finding:
  within those 7, direction and magnitude of change track the region's
  rotation cohort, not the individual region (all 3 LATAM regions decline
  -12% to -18% revenue/active-month between windows; all 3 Europe regions
  grow +14% to +17%; South Asia's outlier -71.9% move is corroborated in
  direction by its nearest cohort sibling) -- consistent with a shared,
  time-varying generation parameter, not independent regional trends.
  Separately finds that every substantial active block, regardless of
  region or calendar year, ramps up over its first month (~48% of that
  block's own average) and tapers down over its last (~77%) -- a mechanical
  fade-in/fade-out pattern, not organic launch/wind-down behavior.
